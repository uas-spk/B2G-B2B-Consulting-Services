Bid protest — what has to be furnished

Filing and case assembly from USD 250 per document, with the rate set after the record is reviewed. Every item below must be supplied, dated, and designated public or confidential so the correct versions can be filed.

A complaint is only as strong as the record behind it. Furnish everything; the designation decides what the world sees.

A · The procurement itself

  • Solicitation in full, with every attachment, annex and drawing
  • All amendments and addenda, in issue order
  • Questions submitted and the buyer’s published answers
  • Bidders’ conference or site-visit records, if any
  • Evaluation criteria, weightings and scoring methodology as published
  • Closing date and time, and proof of the version you bid against

B · Your submission

  • The complete bid as submitted, in the form submitted
  • Portal submission receipt, timestamp and confirmation emails
  • Pricing sheets, rate build-up and any certifications signed
  • Compliance matrix or cross-reference you filed, if any
  • Clarification requests received and your responses
  • Any BAFO or revised offer, with the covering correspondence

C · The award and what followed

  • Award or regret notice, with date received (this starts the clock)
  • Debriefing request, the debriefing record, and your minutes of it
  • Scores or evaluation feedback given, in any form
  • Name of the winning bidder and the awarded value, as published
  • Contract award notice and any published justification
  • All correspondence with the contracting authority, in date order

D · Evidence supporting the grounds

  • Technical evidence that your offer met the requirement — datasheets, certificates, test reports, drawings
  • Evidence about the winning offer where publicly available (award data, product specifications, certifications)
  • Comparable award history and pricing data for the same buyer or item
  • Standards, codes or regulations relied upon, with the edition cited
  • Witness statements from those who prepared or submitted the bid
  • Anything showing the evaluation departed from its own published criteria

E · Party and capacity

  • Legal name, registration and address of the complainant entity
  • Authority of the person signing the complaint
  • Registration and eligibility status at the time of bidding (portal accounts, set-aside status, certifications)
  • Bid price, for calculating any bid-preparation cost claim
  • Records of time and cost spent preparing the bid

G · How the per-document rate is set

  • From USD 250 — a straightforward document: short record, one or two grounds, little or no redaction
  • Mid — multiple grounds, a substantial evidence bundle, public and confidential versions throughout, third-party information to identify
  • Complex — contested designations, expert or technical evidence to marshal, replies to the institution’s response, or a compressed filing deadline
  • Drivers: number of grounds · volume and state of the evidence · forum filing rules · redaction and designation load · time remaining before the deadline
  • The rate for each document is quoted in writing before it is drafted, and nothing is filed on an unagreed price
  • Where a document turns out simpler than expected, the lower rate applies — the estimate is a ceiling, not a floor

F · Designation — every document, every time

  • Public version — filed on the open record and visible to the other party and the world
  • Confidential version — filed for the forum only, with the confidential passages marked
  • Redaction applied consistently across both versions, page by page
  • Third-party commercial information identified before filing, not after
  • A schedule listing every document, its date, its source and its designation
  • Where a designation is arguable, it is flagged to you before anything is filed

Deadlines are short and strict. The right to complain is usually lost within days of knowing the grounds, so the assessment comes first — USD 100 — and establishes the filing date before assembly begins. Consulting support, not legal representation: you remain the party of record.
