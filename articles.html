Bid/No-Bid: the ten-minute discipline that saves proposal budgets

10 September 2026 · Umer Siddiqui

Most proposal money is lost before a word is written — spent on pursuits that were never winnable. The cure is a scored Bid/No-Bid gate applied to every opportunity, without exception, before any drafting begins.

Five questions do most of the work:

  • Did we know about this requirement before the solicitation published? (If the first time you hear of a bid is the bid itself, someone else shaped it.)
  • Is there an incumbent, and what is their performance record?
  • Can we meet every mandatory requirement today — not "with a partner we haven't found yet"?
  • Does our price posture fit the buyer's history? (Award data tells you what this buyer actually pays.)
  • Do we have the past performance the evaluators are told to score?

Score each honestly, weight them, and set a threshold below which you do not bid — whatever the excitement in the room. Over a year, the bids you decline fund the ones you win: the same proposal budget concentrated on half the pursuits roughly doubles the effort behind each.

Takeaway: the cheapest proposal is the one you correctly never wrote.
