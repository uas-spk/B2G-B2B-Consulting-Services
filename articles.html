Five ways a “brand name or equal” quote gets thrown out before anyone reads your price

14 September 2026 · Umer Siddiqui

A live Army solicitation for commercial refrigeration — five CLINs, a total small-business set-aside, thirty-day preferred delivery — is a perfect teaching case. The technical content is simple. The disqualification traps are not, and every one of them sits outside the specification.

1. The delivery basis. The RFQ says F.O.B. Destination. Quote F.O.B. Shipping Point and the offer is rejected as nonresponsive — not scored lower, rejected. Freight-inclusive pricing is a compliance requirement, not a commercial preference.

2. Silence on “or equal”. If you offer an alternate and do not say so, naming brand, make and model, the government is entitled to hold you to delivering the exact brands listed. Silence becomes a commitment you never intended.

3. Missing literature. The contracting officer does not go looking for evidence that your product qualifies. No data sheets, drawings or illustrations attached means the salient characteristics cannot be verified, and unverified means non-compliant.

4. One missed salient characteristic. Only the listed characteristics are evaluated — but all of them are. Miss a single dimensional envelope, plug configuration, amperage, refrigerant type or temperature range and the quote fails, however good the rest of it is. Refrigerant is the quiet killer on this class of equipment: R-290 and R-450A are not interchangeable just because both are “low-GWP”.

5. The unsigned certification. Buy American / Balance of Payments certifications must be returned, filled in, with country of origin identified. Submitted blank, or quoting a product from a non-qualifying country, the bid is rejected immediately. Add the current supply-chain prohibitions — covered telecommunications and video-surveillance equipment, the covered-application ban — and the paperwork carries as much rejection risk as the engineering.

Then there is the mechanism itself: electronic submission only, through the government portal, with the amendment-notification option switched on. Emailed or hand-delivered quotes are rejected as nonresponsive, and an amendment you never saw obliges an offer you never submitted.

Takeaway: on commercial-item buys, the specification is rarely what loses the bid. The delivery basis, the certifications, the literature and one overlooked salient characteristic do — which is exactly what a compliance matrix built from the solicitation, clause by clause, is for.

Bid/No-Bid: the ten-minute discipline that saves proposal budgets

10 September 2026 · Umer Siddiqui

Most proposal money is lost before a word is written — spent on pursuits that were never winnable. The cure is a scored Bid/No-Bid gate applied to every opportunity, without exception, before any drafting begins.

Five questions do most of the work:

  • Did we know about this requirement before the solicitation published? (If the first time you hear of a bid is the bid itself, someone else shaped it.)
  • Is there an incumbent, and what is their performance record?
  • Can we meet every mandatory requirement today — not "with a partner we haven't found yet"?
  • Does our price posture fit the buyer's history? (Award data tells you what this buyer actually pays.)
  • Do we have the past performance the evaluators are told to score?

Score each honestly, weight them, and set a threshold below which you do not bid — whatever the excitement in the room. Over a year, the bids you decline fund the ones you win: the same proposal budget concentrated on half the pursuits roughly doubles the effort behind each.

Takeaway: the cheapest proposal is the one you correctly never wrote.
