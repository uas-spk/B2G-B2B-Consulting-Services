Bid readiness assessment

Twenty-two questions that decide the tender — asked before you spend a dollar on the proposal.

Twenty-two questions across the seven things that decide a tender before a word of the proposal is written. Answer honestly — the result is a readiness score, a gap list and the sensible next step. Nothing is stored on this site.

The seven pillars scored by the assessment below.

1 · Eligibility & registrations

Pass/fail items. If any of these fail, no proposal can win — so they come first.

Are your portal registrations active and current (SAM.gov/UEI/CAGE, CanadaBuys, MERX, Ariba/Jaggaer, UNGM, GCC portals)?

Is your size standard or set-aside status established (small business, 8(a), WOSB/SDVOSB, Indigenous, local content)?

Do you hold the management certifications buyers ask for (ISO 9001, AS9100, ISO 27001, others)?

Are insurance limits and bonding capacity (bid, performance, payment) available at the level tenders require?

Where required: personnel or facility security clearance, JCP certification, export-control registration?

In your target market: local entity, agent, commercial registration or licence where the buyer requires one?

2 · Past performance

What you can evidence, not what you have done.

Can you evidence contracts of comparable scope, value and recency?

Are reference letters, completion certificates or performance ratings (CPARS / vendor ratings) available?

Do you have referees willing to be contacted, with consent?

3 · Best-value proposition

Why this buyer should choose you over the incumbent.

Can you state your winning advantage in one sentence, and defend it?

Is your price posture deliberate — you know whether you sit below, at, or above market, and why?

Do you know your weakness in this pursuit and how it has been used against you before?

4 · Team & technical competency

Named personnel requirements are a common silent disqualifier.

Are the CVs of the people who will deliver current and tender-ready?

Can you meet any named key-personnel requirements (years, degrees, certifications, registrations, clearances)?

Do you have a technical author or SME available to write the technical content?

5 · Vendors, OEMs & partners

Where the offer depends on someone else’s product or price.

Are OEM or supplier relationships formal (distributor, authorised reseller, agent) rather than ad hoc?

Where required: letters of authorisation or manufacturer support letters in hand?

Is country-of-origin and supply-chain compliance confirmed (Buy American/Canadian, TAA, NDAA §889, local content)?

Where you need partners: teaming or JV agreements signed, with flow-down terms accepted?

6 · Documents & submission access

What exists today, and how the submission physically happens.

Do you have the full solicitation package including all attachments and amendments?

Is there a content library ready (capability statement, boilerplate, CVs, certifications, financials, references)?

Are product and technical documents ready (datasheets, drawings, certificates, test reports)?

Is submission access arranged — a delegated sub-user account for the consultant, or will you upload the final files yourself?

7 · Qualification matrix

The analysis that decides bid or no-bid.

Have the mandatory (pass/fail) requirements been listed, and can every one be evidenced today?

Do you know which rated criteria carry the most weight, and where you score badly?

Does a requirement-by-requirement compliance matrix already exist for this solicitation?

8 · Your requirement

So the result can be matched to the right starting point.

What do you need most?

Answer at least the eligibility and qualification-matrix sections to get a meaningful result.
